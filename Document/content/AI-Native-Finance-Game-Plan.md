## 2. Compliance reality (the only section that can actually stop you)

### 2.1 Immigration — resolved
Green card = you can run a side business, take outside income, and monetize active services. The pages of USCIS/H-1B caution in your old memos no longer apply. Remove them from your thinking.

### 2.2 The real gate: Barclays + FINRA
- **If you are FINRA-registered:** Rule 3270 requires **prior written notice to Barclays before *any* outside business activity — paid or unpaid** — and Barclays then decides whether to permit, condition, or prohibit it. The test the firm applies is whether the activity (1) interferes with your duties or (2) could be seen by clients/public as part of Barclays' business.
- **If you are *not* registered** (likely, given an AI/quant/research role): Barclays still has internal outside-business-activity, conflicts, personal-account-dealing, and "speaking in a personal capacity" policies. You must clear it internally either way.
- A proposed replacement (Rule 3290) would narrow disclosure to "investment-related" activities only — **but it is not in effect.** Assume current rules.

### 2.3 The tension nobody flagged
The **fastest-money path** (selling AI advice *to investment firms*) is also the **highest-conflict path**, because you'd be selling into the same buyer universe Barclays serves, while employed there. The **cleanest path** is selling **education to individuals** (library, cohort, paid newsletter, recorded course). It's slightly slower but far easier to get approved and far safer.

### 2.4 What to actually do — this week
1. **File the OBA / outside-activity disclosure with Barclays Compliance now**, framed as: *"Personal-capacity educational content and digital products about AI in finance — no investment advice, no client solicitation, no use of Barclays name/data/clients/resources, views my own."* File before you earn a dollar.
2. Get the firm's answer in writing. Build only inside what they approve.
3. Hard rules regardless: no investment recommendations, no trading signals, no client-specific advice, no Barclays IP/data/branding, clear "views my own" disclaimer everywhere.
4. If registered, a 30-min call with a securities-compliance attorney is cheap insurance before you take high-ticket firm engagements (Phase 2).

> **Design principle:** Phase 1 sells *education to individuals* (clean). Phase 2 adds *briefings/workshops via partners* (cleaner than direct firm consulting). Direct firm advisory is last and most selective.

## 7. The content engine — 9 non-basic pieces (with real angles)

These are deliberately above the noise. Each has a thesis sharp enough that a competitor's "AI is transforming finance" post looks empty next to it. Each ships with a paid artifact.

**1. "The Seven Silent Failure Modes of Finance AI"**
Thesis: the dangerous failures are the ones that *look* right. Taxonomy: point-in-time leakage, look-ahead bias injected by retrieval, table/number corruption in 10-K extraction, citation hallucination in memos, eval blindness, tool-use overreach, governance/data leakage. *Artifact: the 7-failure-mode audit scorecard.*

**2. "Why Output Monitoring Can't Catch a Confidently Wrong Investment Memo"** ← your moat piece
Thesis: checking outputs is necessary and insufficient; you need to look at what the model is doing internally. Introduce activation probes as a runtime risk gate. This is the piece *only you* can write. *Artifact: runtime risk-gate reference architecture.*

**3. "RAG Over Filings Is a Look-Ahead-Bias Machine"**
Thesis: naive RAG over PDFs silently injects future information into backward-looking analysis — a quant-specific failure most AI-finance writers don't even know exists. *Artifact: point-in-time retrieval checklist.*

**4. "Table Extraction Is the Real Bottleneck in Document Intelligence (Not the LLM)"**
Thesis: one mis-parsed cell in a 10-Q quietly corrupts every downstream number; layout-aware extraction + validation matters more than model choice. *Artifact: extraction benchmark template + vendor matrix.*

**5. "Agentic Finance Is Bounded Delegation, Not Autonomy"**
Thesis: the IPS (Investment Policy Statement) should become the control layer; every agent needs a risk gate before tool use. *Artifact: agent risk-gate checklist + governance policy template.*

**6. "An Eval Harness for Investment Memos"**
Thesis: you can't ship what you can't measure; here's how to score memo faithfulness, sourcing, and hallucination quantitatively. Deeply practical, almost nobody publishes this. *Artifact: memo eval harness notebook.*

**7. "The AI-Native Investment Firm Maturity Model"** — your manifesto
Thesis: most firms aren't at level 1; they have a *collection of pilots*, not a strategy. *Artifact: the maturity self-assessment.*

**8. "Quant AI Is Not AI Stock Picking"**
Thesis: serious financial AI is research, validation, risk, and workflow — backtesting pitfalls, leakage, model validation. Ties directly to your O'Reilly book. *Artifact: model-validation checklist.*

**9. "What Mechanistic Interpretability Teaches Us About Trusting Finance Agents"** ← moat + thought-leadership
Thesis: translate your Mechanic/Repair research into a finance-operator lesson on why internal signals beat behavioral signals for risk detection. This is the piece that gets you on stages and into VC conversations. *Artifact: none needed — this one builds brand, not the library.*

**Cadence:** one deep piece every 1–2 weeks (you), everything else is repurposing (marketing person). 80% practical/operator content, 20% deep-tech credibility. Don't invert that ratio.
