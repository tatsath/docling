## The 2–3 minute edit — shot by shot

Target **2:30–3:00**. Structure it like a movie trailer, not a greatest-hits compilation.

**0:00–0:10 — Cold open (the 15-second test).**
Your single best line, delivered on stage. Pull the punchiest 8–10 seconds from "Vibe Banking" or the interpretability talk — a contrarian hook or a "here's what everyone gets wrong" moment. No logo, no music swell first, no slow b-roll. Start on your voice and energy. This is the clip that decides whether they keep watching.

**0:10–0:18 — Name card.**
Lower-third or quick full card: *"Hariom Tatsat — AI Trust & Interpretability · O'Reilly Author."* Then straight back to footage.

**0:18–1:30 — The substance (intercut the two stage talks).**
3–4 tight segments, 12–20 sec each, alternating between the two stage talks. Prioritize: a sharp thesis, one framework moment, and a clip where the *audience is visible* (reaction shots sell "people book this person"). Flash a venue lower-third as each clip's source appears (AI4, AI Summit NY, etc.).

**1:30–2:05 — Range + command (GTC supplements here).**
Short cutaway from the GTC webinar with the NVIDIA mark on screen (shows the logo without anchoring on webinar footage), then the **GTC Q&A**: 2–3 of your crispest question-answers, fast cuts. This proves you can think on your feet, not just deliver a script.

**2:05–2:25 — Build.**
Faster cuts of one-line highlights across all talks — escalating energy, music lifts.

**2:25–2:40 — Logo + invite collage.**
The conference graphics, badges, "I'm Speaking" banners, and stage photos as a clean animated collage: *Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta · NVIDIA GTC · AI4 · The AI Summit New York · Reuters Momentum · NexGen.*

**2:40–2:55 — Close card.**
*"Hariom Tatsat · Keynotes & Executive Briefings · htatsat.com/speaking"* + email. Hold 3 seconds.

**Style:** clean, editorial, finance-credible (not flashy-YouTuber). Light captions on key lines for sound-off viewing. One understated royalty-free track under everything. Color-correct so all sources match. Music keeps the pace up through the whole reel.

## Where to host it (the part you asked about)

Do **all three** — they serve different purposes:

1. **YouTube (unlisted) — your master link.** Upload the finished reel as *unlisted* (not private, not public). Unlisted = anyone with the link can watch, but it won't clutter your channel or show in search. This is the link you paste into bureau forms and emails. Universal, no download needed, plays on any device.
2. **Embedded on htatsat.com/speaking — the hero.** Embed that same YouTube video at the top of your Speaking page so click-throughs see it immediately.
3. **Keep the MP4 file** (1080p) on hand for the few bureau forms that ask for a direct upload. Most want a *link*, not a file — but a couple of self-serve platforms (eSpeakers) let you upload the file, so have it ready.

**On the bureau inquiry/submission forms:** paste the **YouTube unlisted link** in the "video / demo reel" field. If a form has both a link field and an upload, use the link; upload the MP4 only if link isn't an option. Never email a raw MP4 as an attachment (size + spam filters) — always send the link.

So: **YouTube unlisted link is your primary; website embed is your shopfront; MP4 is your backup.**

## Also cut these while the editor is in there (cheap add-on)
Ask for **five 9:16 vertical clips, 45–90 sec each, captioned** — one idea per clip — from the same footage. These are for LinkedIn (3 posts/week) and double as proof. Same raw material, near-zero extra cost since the editor already has the project open.

## How the top speakers structure theirs (what to tell your editor to emulate)
- **Trailer, not highlight reel.** The biggest mistake is stringing clips together; a great reel is an engineered narrative — open with the strongest moment, build, close on credibility.
- **Hook in the first 5–15 seconds.** Planners decide in the first 15 seconds.
- **Live stage anchors; webinar supplements.** Live footage builds credibility nothing else fakes.
- **2–3 minutes, music-driven pace.** The consistent industry standard is 2–3 minutes.
- **You don't need cinematic production.** People are convinced by your expertise, not how slick the reel is; start with whatever footage you have — content and energy matter more than equipment. A good editor delivers this for **under ~$1,000**, often far less on Fiverr/Upwork.

To see real examples before you brief anyone, search YouTube for **"keynote speaker demo reel"** and **"AI keynote speaker sizzle reel"**, and look at how speakers on **SpeakAbout AI** and **BigSpeak** rosters open and close theirs — note the cold-open hook and the logo collage at the end. That's the pattern you're matching.
