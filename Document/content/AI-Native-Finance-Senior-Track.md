# Senior Track — The High-Authority Game Plan
### Repositioning from "creator economics" to "the person institutions fly in"

> Companion to the main game plan. Where the first document built a ladder from $0 up, this one **deletes the bottom of the ladder** and runs a different machine entirely. Read this as the override for anything in the first doc that signals "creator."

---

## 1. The core shift: two different machines

You instinctively flinched at the influencer model, and you're right to. They are not the same business scaled — they're opposite businesses.

| | Creator machine | **Senior-authority machine (your target)** |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue shape | Many small transactions | **A few large transactions** |
| Sold on | Audience size, volume | **Proof assets + category ownership** |
| Hero product | $199 pack, $499 library | **$15–40K keynote / briefing / program** |
| Content | High volume (daily/3x week) | **Low volume, very high substance** |
| Acquisition | Inbound from content treadmill | **Speaker bureaus + institutional channel** |
| Price psychology | Compete down | **Price signals seniority — compete up** |
| Time fit (you have 5–7h/wk) | Bad (ops-heavy) | **Good (few high-leverage moves)** |

**Why higher is *easier* for you specifically:** one $25K keynote equals ~125 sales of a $199 pack. Fewer units, almost no operations, less of your scarce time, and senior buyers are *less* price-sensitive — they read price as a quality signal. You already hold the assets this machine runs on (book, marquee stages, research). The work is packaging, not earning credibility from scratch.

- ❌ Beta cohorts at $750 for analysts. If you run a program, it's a **senior leadership masterclass**, small and priced like one.

## 5. The category to OWN (this is what justifies the fee)

Generalists get $5K. **Category-owners get $40K.** Don't be "an AI in finance speaker" — own one defensible idea nobody senior is saying *from the inside*. Yours is sitting right there in your moat:

> ## "Can you trust an AI that manages money?"
> ### The trust gap in financial AI — and the risk gates that close it.

This fuses your interpretability research + agent failure modes + 15 years quant + the bank seat. It's executive-relevant (boards are terrified of exactly this), it's contrarian-credible (you can say *why output monitoring isn't enough* and back it with real internals work), and **no senior finance figure is currently claiming it.** Plant your flag there.

**Own a proprietary framework + an index** (frameworks travel; indices get cited annually and create a recurring news hook):
- **The Finance AI Risk-Gate Stack™** — retrieval / extraction / reasoning-eval / runtime-probe / human-approval gates.
- **The AI Trust Index for Financial Institutions™** — an annual, named benchmark you publish. This alone can become a yearly press moment, a keynote, and a report.

## 6. The single highest-leverage move: a flagship report / second book

Every template above is anchored by a defining publication. You already have an O'Reilly book — but it's a technical reference, not an *executive* artifact. Create the executive-facing flagship:

> **"The AI-Native Investment Firm: An Executive Field Guide"** — a definitive 40–80 page report (or your second, exec-facing book).

This does four things at once: it's the inbound magnet that makes bureaus want you, the reason conferences pay (not just invite) you, the spine of the keynote, and the credibility that lets you price briefings at $25K+. Start it in parallel from week one.

## 7. The channel — how senior engagements actually get sold

You won't grind outbound DMs. You build a channel:

1. **Upgrade where you already are.** You're a listed AI-in-Finance Summit speaker. Go back to the programming chair and convert "panelist" into **paid keynote** using your signature talk + reel. Same for any Fed/GTC/AI4 contacts.
2. **Get bureau representation.** Bureaus take ~25–30% but bring inbound and *set your fee floor*. Targets: technology/innovation-thought-leader firms (e.g., Stern Strategy — represents Azeem Azhar), plus Leading Authorities, BigSpeak, APB, Washington Speakers Bureau, Gotham Artists. **They won't take you until you have a one-sheet, a reel, a signature talk, and a paid gig or two** — so manufacture those first (§8).
3. **Corporate buyers for programs/briefings:** Chief AI Officers, heads of innovation, L&D/exec-education teams, "AI councils" at banks/asset managers/insurers. These buy $30–75K programs.
4. **Credibility multipliers:** industry councils (WEF-style), exec-ed teaching slots (your CFA/MFE network), and senior podcasts — *not* creator pods. Aim for Exponential View, a16z, Bloomberg/Goldman/McKinsey shows, CFA Institute. These borrow institutional audiences, not hobbyist ones.

## 8. The 30 / 60 / 90 plan (senior version)

The first month is **proof-asset manufacturing**, not check-chasing. You can't sell the $40K talk until the artifacts exist.

### Days 1–10 — Reposition + clear
- Confirm/file the Barclays OBA, framed narrowly: **personal-capacity speaking and authorship; no investment advice, no client solicitation, no firm data/branding.** Speaking-in-personal-capacity is the *cleanest* outside activity — this model is more compliance-friendly than selling to firms.
- Lock the category line ("Can you trust an AI that manages money?") and the two framework/index names.
- Rewrite your bio and site to **peer-of-buyers** register: O'Reilly author, marquee stages, research — strip anything creator-flavored.

### Days 10–25 — Build the proof assets (the unlock)
- **Speaker one-sheet:** signature talk title + 3 takeaways, bio, marquee-logo strip (Fed Atlanta, NVIDIA GTC, AI4, AI Summit NY), topics, testimonials, contact.
- **Signature keynote:** full narrative arc built on the Risk-Gate framework + the Trust Index reveal. This is the asset everything else hangs on.
- **Sizzle reel (3–5 min):** cut from existing stage footage if available; otherwise record one polished studio delivery. Bureaus require this.
- **Begin the flagship report** (§6). Outline + first sections.

### Days 25–45 — Activate the channel
- Convert the AI-in-Finance Summit relationship to a **paid keynote** ask; pitch 5–8 senior conferences/industry summits with the one-sheet + reel.
- Submit to 2–3 speaker bureaus.
- Line up 2–3 **senior** podcast/exec-briefing appearances (institutional, not creator).
- Seed the premium-briefing list with a single high-substance public essay (the manifesto), not a posting blitz.

### Days 45–90 — Convert + compound
- Deliver 1–2 paid keynotes/briefings ($15–40K) — even one at a marquee venue resets your fee floor.
- Publish (or pre-release) the flagship report; use it to open corporate-program and advisory conversations.
- First bureau on contract or in active conversation.
- 1 corporate masterclass / board-briefing lead in pipeline.

## 9. Honest caveats (so you don't over-rotate)

- **Senior *positioning* you can claim now; top-tier *fees* ramp.** $15–25K is reachable in 90 days with the assets above. $40K+ typically needs a bureau + the flagship publication + sustained visibility — a 12–24 month build.
- **Going senior ≠ doing less substance — it means *more* substance at *lower* volume.** The depth of your point of view is exactly what justifies the price. You can't skip the thinking, only the noise.
- **A bureau won't touch you empty-handed.** Reel + one-sheet + signature talk + a paid gig come first. Month 1 exists to manufacture these.
- **The cleanest path is speaking/authorship in personal capacity.** The moment you cross into advising specific firms or anything advice-adjacent, the Barclays/FINRA conflict tightens — keep that for selective, Compliance-cleared, inbound-only engagements.

## 10. One-line summary

**Stop building a ladder up from $0. Plant a flag on one ownable idea — "can you trust an AI that manages money?" — manufacture the proof assets (signature keynote, reel, one-sheet, flagship report), get a bureau, and sell a handful of $15–40K keynotes, board briefings, and leadership programs a year. One engagement a month is your number; the book/report and the Trust Index make each one easier and more expensive than the last.**
